# Instructor Feedback on Initial Dataset Submission

Overall, this initial dataset is a strong start, and there's a lot here to build on. I like that you've organized the repository thoughtfully, having a folder for the initial dataset with its own `README.md` and then a separate `README.md` for the project as a whole is good practice and shows you're thinking about how others will navigate your work. I also appreciate the explicit note on the Audit and Augment approach, the clear explanation of how Python was used to generate the new fields, and the transparency about your tools.

That said, there are several things I want to push back on, both in terms of the submission itself and in terms of earlier feedback that hasn't quite been addressed yet.

**1. The Existing Dataset Hasn't Been Audited**

The most significant issue is that the Audit and Augment approach is supposed to start with the audit, and that work hasn't happened here. In my earlier feedback, I indicated that you would need to trace the data back to its source, spot-check rows, understand how it was compiled, and identify gaps, particularly around early esports history where documentation tends to be thinner. This work is similar to what we did with the HathiTrust dataset in class, and esportsearnings.com is still online, which means you actually have the opportunity to do this verification work directly.

None of it appears in the documentation. The `README.md` says you "began with an existing esports dataset," but it doesn't link to it, describe where it came from, or say anything meaningful about its origins or limitations beyond a vague note that it "lacked some cultural metadata." Without knowing who created `GeneralEsportData.csv`, what it covers, what it excludes, and whether its numbers hold up against the source, any augmentation built on top of it is on uncertain ground. Some specific questions worth investigating: does the dataset include all games on the site, or only a subset? Are certain regions, platforms, or tournament types over- or under-represented? How has data collection changed over the 1998–2023 span, given that early esports tournaments are notoriously underdocumented compared to recent ones? I previously mentioned you might utilize the web archive to investigate this website, and I would highly encourage you to at least attempt some verification, since otherwise it is not clear how you are auditing the dataset.

**2. The Augmentation Needs More Cultural Depth**

Related to this question of verification, I want to push you to consider what the three new fields ( `RegionPopularity`, `CompetitiveLevel`, and `Notes` ) are actually doing for your dataset. While I commend you for using Python and sharing your scripts (though both lacked correct file extenions), I remain concerned that the augmentation doesn't add much cultural depth and that it essentially confirms what the original data already showed: that the most popular games have the most prize money and tournaments, and that those games are concentrated in the wealthiest regions. That's not a surprising finding, and it raises the question of what the augmentation is actually adding. In your initial topic description, you seemed to be interested in questions of inequality and cultural attention, but the new fields don't really get at those concepts in a meaningful way.

So, I think you have a larger question to answer which is why are you creating this data and to what ends are you hoping to achieve with it? The original dataset is already telling us something about the economics of esports, but what is the cultural story you want to tell with this data?

You write in the `README.md` that you chose esports because it "reflects how digital entertainment becomes professionalized and monetized," and there's a lot of promise in that framing, but you need to work on getting the data to actually speak to that topic. You might consider augmenting not with this existing dataset, but, like you mentioned, combining with other datasets that might help you get at the regional focus a bit more. Again, actually verifying the dataset might also give you an angle about how esports became professionalized over time. Generally though, the computational work is solid, but it needs to be paired with more interpretive depth to really make this a strong submission.

**3. The Findings and Next Steps Need More Specificity**

I like the idea in the next steps plan of bringing in additional datasets to expand the analysis, but it's a bit unclear to me how web scraping or APIs will help here specifically. As I stated above, the more pressing question is what you're actually trying to get at. You identified inequality between games, genre popularity, and cultural attention as your core interests, but the documentation doesn't engage with how you would operationalize any of those. Perhaps your topic has changed? That's completely acceptable but it would be helpful to note that in your documentation. And of course, you need to then specify your current focus a bit more as well.

As a small suggestion, I would also recommend adding a table to the `README.md` for the dataset fields with data types and examples, which would make the dataset much more legible to outside readers. 

Finally, I'd encourage you to start engaging with some of the scholarship I mentioned in my earlier feedback. T.L. Taylor's *Raising the Stakes* would be a strong starting point for framing the professionalization angle you're circling around, and there's growing work in journals like *Games and Culture* and *New Media & Society* that would help you situate your findings in a broader conversation. That engagement will be required for the final data essay, so the earlier you start the better.

Overall, I can see that you are starting to work with this data in-depth: your code works, the genre-balanced sampling is a thoughtful structural choice, and the topic has genuine cultural depth that I think you can develop meaningfully. The main thing holding this back right now is that you still need to verify the data and explore its intrancices, as well as consider how augmentation can push the new fields toward genuine cultural insight rather than algorithmic defaults, and finally, thinking more carefully about what complexity you're actually trying to surface will make this a much stronger submission. Happy to chat more as you move into the next phase!
